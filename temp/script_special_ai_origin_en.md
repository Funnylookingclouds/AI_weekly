| Hook | When was AI first mentioned? Spoiler: way before ChatGPT. |
| 1955 – the name | **August 31st, 1955.** A young mathematician, **John McCarthy**, writes a funding proposal with **Marvin Minsky**, **Nathaniel Rochester** and **Claude Shannon**. And right in the title: a brand-new term – **artificial intelligence**. |
| 1956 – Dartmouth | The plan: a **two-month, ten-person** summer workshop at **Dartmouth College** in **1956**. Their bold idea: every feature of intelligence can be described so precisely that a machine could simulate it. |
| 1950 – the question | But the question is even older. In **1950**, **Alan Turing** asked: "Can machines think?" – and proposed the **imitation game**, today's **Turing test**. |
| Ending | Seventy-one years later, that idea fits in your pocket. |

## Facts & sources
- Proposal dated Aug 31, 1955, signed by McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester, Shannon; title "A Proposal for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence"; asks for "a 2 month, 10 man study"; OED cites it as first use of the term.
  - https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/1904
  - https://home.dartmouth.edu/about/artificial-intelligence-ai-coined-dartmouth
  - https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=61238
- Key sentence (paraphrased in script): "every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it."
- Turing, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence", Mind 59 (236), Oct 1950: opens with "I propose to consider the question, 'Can machines think?'" and introduces the imitation game.
  - https://web.mit.edu/practicum/www/turingab.htm
- 1955 → 2026 = 71 years.
