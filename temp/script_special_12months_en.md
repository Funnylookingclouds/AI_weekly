| Hook | Rune | How fast is AI really moving? Let's measure twelve months. |
| Capability | Ture | Researchers at **METR** track how long a task – in human working time – AI can complete half the time. **November 2025: about three hours.** **This spring: around twelve.** That length **doubles** roughly every **three and a half months**. That's about **ten times longer every year**. |

## Facts & sources
- METR "50% time horizon" = length of tasks (measured by how long they take human experts) that AI agents complete with 50% success.
  - Nov 2025: GPT-5.1-Codex-Max ≈ 173 min (METR TH1.1 re-estimate; original report 75–350 min range; widely reported as 2 h 42 min) → "about three hours".
    https://metr.org/evaluations/gpt-5-1-codex-max-report/ · https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/
  - Spring 2026: public frontier ≈ 12 h (95% CI 5–61 h), METR Frontier Risk Report (May 19, 2026) → "around twelve". https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/
  - Doubling time ≈ 3.6 months (95% CI 2.1–4.9), METR Q4 2026 capability update (Nov 20, 2026). 2^(12/3.6) ≈ 10× per year. https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/
  - Caveat: wide confidence intervals; measured on software/research tasks, not all of "intelligence".
- Epoch AI, "The plunging price of thought" (Sep 2026): cost of a fixed performance level falls ~47%/quarter ≈ 13× per year (since 2023). https://epoch.ai/publications/the-plunging-price-of-thought
- Real-world examples from our own recent episodes: OpenAI's unreviewed math proof claims (Oct 26, wk 44); Riemann claim (Nov 20, wk 47); AI vs 8 brain tumor specialists, 97% (AMP 2026, wk 46).
- Not used: Stanford AI Index 2026 figures (couldn't verify against Stanford's own page from here).
